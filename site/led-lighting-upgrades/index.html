LED parking lot lighting upgrades

Old metal halide heads are dim, orange and expensive to run. LED fixtures light the lot evenly and often use half the power or less.

Why upgrade

  • Lower power bills. LED often cuts lighting wattage by half or more.
  • Far fewer repairs. LED fixtures last many years longer than metal halide lamps.
  • Better light. Whiter, more even light with fewer dark spots between poles.
  • Less glare. Modern fixtures aim light at the lot, not at neighbors or the road.

What's included

  • A count of every pole head and wall pack, and what each one should be replaced with
  • LED fixtures mounted on your existing poles and arms in most cases
  • Photocells or timers replaced so the new lights come on when they should
  • Old fixtures hauled away

Rebates for LED upgrades. If your building is a ComEd customer, replacing older fixtures with LED may qualify for an incentive through the ComEd Energy Efficiency Program, which can take a real bite out of the cost. Ask when you request your quote and we'll help you check. Naperville runs its own electric utility, so Naperville properties should check with the city.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth upgrading parking lot lights to LED?

Usually, yes. LED fixtures often use half the power or less of the metal halide heads they replace, last far longer, and spread light more evenly, so the lot looks brighter with fewer dark spots. If your building is a ComEd customer, the upgrade may qualify for an incentive through the ComEd Energy Efficiency Program. (Naperville runs its own electric utility.)

How much does an LED parking lot light cost?

Replacing a pole-mounted head with an LED fixture runs $700–$1,200 per fixture installed, including the lift, and an LED wall pack is $350–$550. Any ComEd incentive you qualify for comes off that.

Should I repair my old lights or switch to LED?

If one or two heads are out on a lot that's otherwise fine, a repair is usually the cheaper fix. If several are failing, or you're replacing lamps and ballasts every year, switching to LED usually costs less over a few years because the fixtures last much longer and use less power.

Do I have to replace the poles to go LED?

Usually not. Most LED fixtures mount on existing poles and arms, and many lots keep the same wiring. We check pole condition while we're up there.

Will LED lights be too bright for neighbors?

They don't have to be. Modern LED fixtures aim light down and onto the lot, and shielded fixtures cut glare toward homes and roads. Some towns have rules on light spilling past the property line, and we pick fixtures to suit.
